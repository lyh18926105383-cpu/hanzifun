How to use 快: speed, “soon,” and a useful contrast with 块

The quickest useful distinction is that 快, kuài, often describes how fast something happens, while 块, also kuài, often counts pieces or units of money. Hearing the sound alone will not tell you which written character you need. Look at the words around it and ask what the sentence is doing. A person walking fast, a train arriving soon, and a price of six yuan can all contain the sound kuài, but they do not express the same idea.

Start with a phrase rather than one English translation

Learn 很快, hěn kuài, as “fast” or “quickly” in an appropriate sentence. Do not assume that every English use of “quick” can be replaced by 快. Chinese words combine in their own patterns. In 他走得很快, the phrase after 得 describes the way he walks. The whole sentence means “He walks very fast.” If you are describing a train, a response or someone's progress, the surrounding words establish what is fast.

For a first practice session, keep one action and change only the speed description. Compare someone walking quickly with someone walking slowly. This gives 快 a clear job in your sentence. It is more reliable than memorizing a long list of English glosses without knowing where any of them belongs.

Three everyday examples to say and change

他走得很快。

Tā zǒu de hěn kuài.

He walks very fast. Imagine pointing out a friend ahead of you on the street. 得 introduces the description of the walking. Replace 他 with a person's name, but keep the rest of the pattern until it feels familiar.

{{audio:他走得很快。}}

火车快到了。

Huǒchē kuài dào le.

The train will arrive soon. Here 快 does not mean the train is moving at high speed. Together with 到了, it puts the arrival close to the present. A learner who translates each character separately may miss this difference. Picture waiting on a platform and checking an approaching arrival.

{{audio:火车快到了。}}

请说慢一点，我听不懂。

Qǐng shuō màn yì diǎn, wǒ tīng bu dǒng.

Please speak a little more slowly; I don't understand. This contrasting sentence gives you a useful response when someone's speech is too fast. The word for slow is 慢, màn. You can use the request without criticizing the speaker or pretending you understood.

Read the components without inventing an origin story

In the modern written form, 快 has the narrow component 忄 on the left and 夬 on the right. The left component is associated with 心 in character learning and dictionary classification. Use that visible shape to distinguish 快 from another character with the same right side. A component can help you notice a family resemblance, but it does not give you permission to invent an ancient story or assume a modern meaning from appearance alone.

Here is an original memory association: imagine a person feeling impatient while waiting for a slow lift. The narrow left side reminds you to notice that feeling; the whole character cues “quick.” This is deliberately a memory cue, not a claim about how 快 developed historically. If the story does not help you, use a clear image of someone walking quickly instead.

快 and 块: the left side changes the word

Compare 块 with 快. Both use kuài with a fourth tone, and both have 夬 on the right in their modern forms. 块 has 土 on the left. In 六块钱, liù kuài qián, 块 expresses a unit of money in ordinary speech: six yuan. In 一块蛋糕 it counts a piece of cake. Neither expression describes speed.

Try a two-column note headed “how fast?” and “how many pieces or yuan?” Put 走得快 under the first and 六块钱 under the second. Then cover the characters and select the correct one from the context. This tests a real reading decision. Copying the two forms ten times without a context may improve your pen control but will not resolve their identical pronunciation.

A short retrieval check

1. Which character belongs in 六__钱?

块: this is an amount of money, not a description of speed.

2. Does 火车快到了 tell you the train's speed?

No. It says the arrival will happen soon.

3. What should you inspect first when 快 and 块 look similar?

The left component, then the phrase around the character.

Save 快 only after you can explain one sentence without looking at its English line. Tomorrow, retrieve both meanings by contrasting a fast action with an event happening soon. Then visit the conversation scenarios and find a moment where a polite request about speed would help.
