Shenzhen may have caught your attention because of APEC 2026. But there is another way to get to know the city: start with its name.

In Chinese, Shenzhen is written 深圳 and pronounced Shēnzhèn. These two characters carry a reminder of fields and waterways, even as the city is known for its modern skyline and electronics industry.

Read on for a short introduction to Shenzhen, then try a two-character Chinese lesson. No previous Chinese needed.

Why is Shenzhen in the APEC spotlight?

The 2026 APEC Economic Leaders’ Meeting is scheduled for November 18–19 in Shenzhen, according to APEC’s official calendar. APEC stands for Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation.

For readers encountering Shenzhen through the event, the city offers a useful starting point for exploring modern China—and a surprisingly memorable first Chinese place name.

Where is Shenzhen?

Shenzhen is in Guangdong Province in southern China, next to Hong Kong. It is a city where international business, manufacturing and everyday urban life meet.

One date helps explain its modern development: 1980, when the Shenzhen Special Economic Zone was established. Its growth since then is an important part of China’s economic reform story. That date marks the special economic zone, however, not the beginning of human settlement in the area.

You do not need to understand that whole history to begin exploring. Start with three different views of the city.

Three ways to see Shenzhen

1. A city connected to electronics

Huaqiangbei is closely associated with Shenzhen’s electronics industry. Its development from an industrial area into an electronics trading district gives visitors one way to understand the city’s reputation for making and exchanging things.

If the skyline shows the scale of Shenzhen, the story of Huaqiangbei helps explain some of the work behind it.

2. A city with room for green space

The photograph of Lianhuashan Park above offers a different view: greenery in front of tall buildings. It is a useful reminder to look beyond the towers when getting to know a city.

Try a small language connection as you look at the scene: 山 — shān — mountain. You do not need to memorise every new word. Noticing one familiar character is a good beginning.

3. A city beside the water

Shenzhen Bay adds another perspective to the urban landscape. Water also gives us a bridge to the city’s name: the characters 深圳 preserve a connection to the area’s older landscape.

What does “Shenzhen” mean?

深deep
＋
圳field channel
→
深圳Shēnzhèn

深 means “deep.” 圳 refers to a ditch or channel in fields. Together, the name is often explained as “deep ditch” or “deep channel.”

The Shenzhen government’s account of the name connects it to a deep water channel in the area’s fields.

That creates a memorable contrast: a city associated with modern technology has a name rooted in land and water. This explains the place name; in everyday English, you would still call the city Shenzhen, rather than translate its name.

Learn 深圳, one character at a time

深shēndeep

  • Tone: first tone—high and level.
  • Strokes: 11 in standard modern writing.
  • Look for: 氵, the water radical on the left.
  • Try a word: 深水 · shēn shuǐ · deep water.

Memory cue: picture water that is too deep to stand in. Use the water radical as a reminder of the meaning. This is a learning aid, not a complete account of the character’s historical origin.

圳zhèna ditch or channel in fields

  • Tone in 深圳: fourth tone—a sharp fall.
  • Strokes: 6.
  • Look for: 土 on the left and 川 on the right.
  • Where to start: recognise 圳 as the second character in 深圳.

Memory cue: imagine a channel carrying water through the earth. It can help you connect the visible parts with the meaning. You do not need to learn 圳 as a common standalone conversation word.

Say the city name: Shēnzhèn

These sketches explain the tones; they are not acoustic measurements.

Keep shēn high and level, then let zhèn fall. Both syllables end in -n, not -ng. The initial sounds sh and zh are different, so listen to a Mandarin model when practising.

The small marks over the vowels show the tones: ē for the level first tone and è for the falling fourth tone. Write the place name together in pinyin: Shēnzhèn.

Use Shenzhen in a sentence

Three Chinese sentences using Shenzhen
ChinesePinyinEnglish
这是深圳。Zhè shì Shēnzhèn.This is Shenzhen.

Choose the sentence that fits you. Read it once with the pinyin, then look back at 深圳 and say the city name on its own.

A 30-second memory check

Cover the pronunciation and meanings above, then try these three questions:

  1. Which character means “deep”—深 or 圳?
  2. Is the first tone in Shēnzhèn level or falling?
  3. What kind of landscape does the name recall?
Reveal the answers
  1. 深 means deep.
  2. Level. Shēn is first tone; zhèn is fourth tone.
  3. Fields and a deep water channel.

If you remembered even one answer, the city’s name is already a little more familiar.

Quick questions about Shenzhen

How do you write Shenzhen in Chinese?

深圳. The first character is 深 and the second is 圳.

How do you pronounce Shenzhen?

In Mandarin, it is Shēnzhèn: first tone followed by fourth tone. Both syllables end in -n.

What does Shenzhen mean?

Its name is commonly explained as “deep ditch” or “deep channel,” reflecting the area’s earlier landscape.

Is Shenzhen the same city as Hong Kong?

No. Shenzhen is a city in Guangdong Province, next to Hong Kong. They are distinct places.

Why is Shenzhen associated with APEC 2026?

Shenzhen is the host city for the 2026 APEC Economic Leaders’ Meeting, scheduled for November 18–19.
