## Mid-Autumn Chinese: Moon, Mooncake and Reunion

Slug: `mid-autumn-festival-chinese-characters`；关联字：中秋月饼团圆；卡组：`mid-autumn`。

Read six characters in three useful festival expressions, then connect them with a small practice set.

### Start with the whole name

The Mid-Autumn Festival is 中秋节, Zhōngqiū Jié. Read the three characters together before separating them: 中 can mean middle, 秋 is autumn, and 节 here means festival. The Ministry of Culture and Tourism’s festival account connects the celebration with reunion and mooncakes. Customs vary between households and regions; treat these as useful cultural connections, rather than a checklist every family follows.

Try a simple reading task: find 中秋 on a greeting or a package, then check which surrounding characters you can explain. Recognizing the festival name in context is a useful first goal. You do not need to translate every ingredient or every line of a greeting to begin.

### Keep lunar and Gregorian dates separate

The festival falls on the fifteenth day of the eighth lunar month. That is not the same as 15 August on the Gregorian calendar. Use the Hong Kong Observatory’s Gregorian–lunar conversion tables to check a particular year. This guide teaches the expressions without advertising an upcoming event or claiming a current holiday schedule.

Make two small labels: 月, yuè, meaning moon or month, and 秋, qiū, meaning autumn. For practice, decide which meaning of 月 fits the expression you are reading. A calendar context and a description of the moon can use the same character differently.

### Read 月饼 as a word

月饼, yuèbǐng, is mooncake. The individual meanings of 月 and 饼 provide a useful first connection, but learn the whole word as the name of the food. 饼 is also used for other cakes or flatbreads; seeing that character alone does not tell you that an item is a mooncake.

Look at the food component 饣 on the left of 饼. Noticing a modern written component can help you recognize a shape. It is not a historical explanation of where the character came from. A memory image of a round cake is an association for study, not evidence about ancient writing.

### Separate 圆 from 团圆

圆, yuán, means round. 团圆, tuányuán, means reunion or being reunited. The festival account links the full moon with reunion. You can use that connection to remember the word without assuming that a literal translation of its two parts explains every use.

Read 月亮很圆, Yuèliang hěn yuán: “The moon is round.” Then read 团圆, tuányuán, as a separate word. Ask yourself which expression describes a shape and which describes people being together. The short sentence is an original teaching example, rather than a quotation from the cultural source.

### A short activity away from the screen

Write 月, 饼 and 圆 on three slips of paper. Put 月 beside 饼 and read the resulting word. Move 圆 beside a drawing of the moon and explain the description aloud. If a partner mixes the slips, rebuild only the expression you understand instead of guessing a new word from any pair.

Open the six linked character pages below to hear available pronunciation and inspect the teaching examples. Save the characters you want to revisit on this device, or practise the Mid-Autumn set. Returning tomorrow to recognize 月饼 in a different layout is a more specific target than trying to memorize an entire festival article.

## Chinese for a First Business Visit: 请, 茶 and 水

Slug: `chinese-for-your-first-business-visit`；关联字：请茶水；卡组：`at-the-tea-house`。

Prepare a courteous greeting and a simple drink exchange, with clear limits on what a short phrase can do.

### Prepare one small exchange

For a first visit, choose a task you can rehearse completely: greet your host, recognize an invitation to sit, and understand an offer of tea or water. This is a language practice plan. A few courteous words do not replace an interpreter for a detailed product discussion, a negotiation or an agreement.

The Canadian Trade Commissioner Service’s business etiquette guide recommends checking the language capabilities of hosts beforehand and arranging interpretation when needed. Use that practical check while preparing your visit. Confirm the people, place and language for the meeting separately from your vocabulary practice.

### Hear an invitation before making one

请坐, qǐng zuò, means “Please sit down.” Here 请 introduces a courteous invitation, and 坐 means to sit. Imagine your host saying it while indicating a chair. If you are the visitor, recognize the invitation; do not automatically repeat it back as though you were offering your host a seat.

The left component 讠 is a useful written feature to notice in 请. That observation helps you distinguish its shape from other characters. Treat a picture or imagined conversation as a memory aid. Do not turn the observation into an unsupported story about the character’s historical origin.

### Understand 茶 and 水 in context

茶, chá, is tea; 水, shuǐ, is water. 茶还是水？, Chá háishi shuǐ?, means “Tea or water?” This is an original example of a choice question. If you only recognize the two drink words at first, pause and ask for clarification instead of pretending you understood the entire exchange.

我想喝茶, Wǒ xiǎng hē chá, means “I would like to drink tea.” Practise the full expression slowly, then identify 茶 when the English line is covered. Do not assume that a single character can describe every drink, ingredient or preference you might need to discuss.

### Practise an alternative you understand

我想喝水, Wǒ xiǎng hē shuǐ, means “I would like to drink water.” Compare it with the tea sentence: only the final word changes. Rehearse both before covering the prompt. A small controlled substitution is easier to check than a newly invented sentence made from several unfamiliar words.

You can read 请给我一杯水, Qǐng gěi wǒ yì bēi shuǐ, as “Please give me a glass of water.” The written 一 keeps its dictionary reading yī; the marked sentence here shows yì before the first-tone classifier 杯. Learn the whole request in the restaurant scenario when you want a longer exchange.

### Connect courtesy with preparation

The Trade Commissioner guide also emphasizes punctuality and clear introductions. Check the actual appointment and say your name clearly. These are preparation points from that guide, not universal predictions about how every colleague will behave. Let your host and the specific setting guide the interaction.

After the meeting, note one expression you recognized and one that needs help. Keep commercial details out of a public vocabulary note. The HanziFun saved-character list stores learning choices in this browser; it is not a contact database or a cross-device meeting record.

### Rehearse, then return to a real scenario

With a partner, take turns offering tea or water and answering with the drink you actually intend. Cover the pinyin for a reading attempt, then uncover it to check. When a phrase feels unfamiliar, inspect its linked character page before increasing the difficulty.

Use the linked tea-house set for individual character recall, and the business icebreaker scenario for complete greetings. These serve different goals: a successful character card does not prove you can follow an unrehearsed conversation. No current trade-fair dates, event registration rules or travel arrangements are claimed in this evergreen guide.

## First Chinese Characters at Home: A Small Shared Activity

Slug: `first-characters-for-bilingual-kids`；关联字：山水木月；卡组：`first-six`。

Try a shared character activity with 山, 水, 木 and 月, using a printable set and a short return visit.

### Choose something you can point to

Start with four characters: 山, shān, mountain; 水, shuǐ, water; 木, mù, tree or wood; and 月, yuè, moon or month. Point to a picture, an object or a familiar view while saying the word. The lexical readings and meanings are checked against the project’s locked CC-CEDICT data. The activities here are original suggestions, not a measured learning programme.

If four items feel like too much, choose two. A shared activity can stop after a useful observation. There is no required age, daily score or promised speed of progress. A family can adapt the words, amount of writing and spoken language to the learner’s needs.

### Notice the shape without inventing an origin

For 山, invite the learner to imagine three peaks. For 木, try a memory picture of a trunk and branches. Label these as memory pictures. They are ways to remember the modern forms, rather than evidence that the current character was created through precisely that story.

For writing, open the stroke-order tool for one selected character and watch a single sequence. Then put the screen aside and try writing it once on a blank space. Compare the result together. If the learner wants to focus on recognition today, matching the written form to a meaning is also a clear task.

### Use a small printed set

The family printable below contains six cards, including these four characters, with pinyin, a meaning and a short memory cue. An adult can cut out the cards. Keep the pinyin visible for a supported reading, or cover it for a recall attempt; say which task you are trying so the activity is understandable.

Place two cards near drawings and invite the learner to match them. Swap their positions for a second attempt. If the answer only works in the original layout, return to the form and meaning instead of treating the first match as secure recognition. This is an activity you can try, not a claim that a particular layout guarantees retention.

### Keep a character and a word distinct

月 can mean moon or month. Its use in a word matters. 木 appears in 树木, shùmù, trees, while 山 appears in 高山, gāoshān, high mountain. Look at an example on a character page and explain the complete word before asking the learner to translate each part.

Do not turn every unfamiliar combination of two cards into a word. If you are uncertain, use the linked dictionary examples and check the expression together. It is fine to say that a combination needs checking. A dependable small example is more useful than an inventive translation that changes the intended meaning.

### Return with one specific question

On a later day, show one card and ask for its meaning before revealing the cue. Alternatively, say the meaning and offer two written choices. These prompts test different things. Choose one for the current attempt, then use the result to decide what to repeat.

You can record an observation such as “recognized 水 without pinyin” instead of assigning a broad ability label. Do not compare one learner’s small set with another’s progress counter. The purpose of the activity is to make a few written forms useful in a shared moment.

Follow any of the character links below to inspect pronunciation, meaning and examples. The linked first-six flashcards add 休 and 火 to the printed activity. An adult can help choose a short practice round and decide which characters to save for another visit.

Saved characters and practice history remain in the current browser on this device. Export a backup before clearing site storage if the record matters to you. This activity needs no child account, shared class list or cloud service. Read the existing study-routine guide if you want to plan an independent follow-up after the shared activity.
